The Only 4 Outbound Metrics That Matter

If you're not tracking your data, you're guessing. These four numbers tell you exactly what's broken.

Jump to the full transcript

Who this is for

Anyone running outbound who wants more calls, more leads and more sales.

Who am I? I'm Dan James. I've signed lots of whale clients with my outreach strategies and got some ridiculous reactions from prospects.

1. AOV (average order value)

A man messaged me on LinkedIn and asked seven questions before admitting he was selling $500 a year software. Four months of manual outreach, no buyers.

$500 a year is about $42 a month. Message 10 people and even if all of them buy, that's $5,000 a year. Not worth the manual work.

  • Low AOV? Keep what it costs to win a customer low: run ads or make content.
  • Outreach works best when each deal is big enough to justify the effort, or when you can raise the order value straight away.

2. Positive reply rate

An early number in the funnel, but it tells you two things:

  • Your lead list is good (one of the most important parts of outbound).
  • Your offer is good. People care and reply "this is great, how can we talk?"

3. Follow-up data

How many times you follow up, and the positive replies you get each time. All of the money is in the follow-up. With creative outbound the reply often comes on the seventh or eighth message.

Track what percentage of prospects get 6+ messages. If it isn't 95% to 100%, you're leaving money on the table.

4. Book rate

The last number in the funnel and the one that matters most: how many calls actually get booked.

Read it last, alongside the others:

  • Bad book rate plus bad follow-up = you're not following up.
  • Good follow-up, good AOV and lots of positive replies, but a bad book rate = something breaks between "yes" and the booking. Are you calling leads fast enough? Is your calendar too full for them to find a time?
