# Best and Worst Outreach Methods for Whale Clients (2027)

Six outreach methods, ranked for signing big clients.

---

# Who this is for

B2B businesses that want to sign large clients and need to know where to put their effort in 2027.

**Who am I?** I'm Dan James. I scaled a previous agency to 40K a month very quickly by signing whale clients, and my whole offer is now doing that for others. We've signed big tech companies like ListKit and Client Ascension, and we book meetings with basically whoever we want.

# The tier list

| Method | Tier | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Physical goods | **S** | 100% response rate for me and my client Connor Benham |
| Creative outreach movie (Super Loom) | **One of my favourites** | Prospects show their partners and families |
| Strategy document | **A** | A business proposal on steroids, a bit more common since AI |

| Cold email | **C** | Anyone can start it, so response rates are plummeting |
| Cold calling | **C, bordering on D** | A volume game, not built for whales |

---

# Cold calling (C, bordering on D)

**Pros:** it works if you keep dialling. You go direct and can reach the decision maker. It's great in some industries, like HVAC and construction, where owners won't hop on a Zoom call.

**Cons:** awful in red-tape industries like finance and law (gatekeeper after gatekeeper). It's manual labour, so it's hard to scale. Constant rejection means high churn.

**Use it for:** volume plays with great processes or an agency. Not for whales, who need something bespoke.

---

# Physical goods (S tier)

Research one prospect (or a few decision makers), find something they'll find fun, and send it to their work address.

**Example:** they love Rafael Nadal, so you send a signed racket: "We hope you love this racket as much as we'd love to work with you."

**Pros:** cuts through the noise like nothing else. It raises their status. Unmatched.

**Cons:** upfront cost (one of the most expensive methods), and you need their work address and some idea of their schedule.

If you can front $500 and sign Netflix or Amazon, you obviously do it.

---

# Cold DMs (B tier)

**Pros:** with a strong profile it's excellent. If Elon Musk DMed you, you'd reply.

**Cons:** you live and die by your profile. A strong profile means you're well known in the space and post a lot of content, not that you've got a few videos up. With a great profile, even bad copy works.

---

# Cold email (C tier)

**Pros:** anyone can start today for around $250: pre-warmed inboxes, domains, AI-written copy.

**Cons:** the same thing. Because anyone can do it, response rates are plummeting. I got five cold emails this morning.

It can sign whales, but it isn't personal, and signing whales is about going slow and making something bespoke.

---

# Creative outreach movie (Super Loom)

You make a movie for one person and send it to them (and you can post it publicly).

**Pros:** response rates are very high. It feels more like Valentine's Day than outreach. People have shown it to their family at Christmas.

**Cons:** it's a lot of work. You need cinematography, lighting, a good camera and good audio. It's cinematic first, business second, which is exactly why it works. Getting them to watch it also takes know-how.

---

# Strategy document (A tier)

Research the prospect like crazy, find their problems, show how you'll solve them, what happens when you do, and why you're the best person for the job. Add real deliverables.

**Pros:** a business proposal on steroids. I've booked hundreds of calls with it, including with the CEO of Seamless AI, who called it "next level engineering."

**Cons:** AI has made it more common, so it doesn't hit as hard as it did 18 months ago.

---

# The pattern

The more effort you put in, the higher the conversion rate. Cold calling takes the least effort and sits at the bottom. Movies and physical goods take the most and sit at the top. If you want whales, put the time in.
