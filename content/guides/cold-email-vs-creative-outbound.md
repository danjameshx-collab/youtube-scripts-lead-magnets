# The two ways to do outbound

**Cold email = going fishing.** Build a list of up to 500,000 potential buyers and blast it, personalising at scale. It is a volume game: email 500,000 people, get 500 replies, 50 calls, 5 clients.

**Creative outbound = whale hunting.** Also called account-based marketing (ABM). You go slow, one prospect at a time, and give each one its own campaign: deep research, a physical gift, an outreach movie, a huge strategy doc, turning up at an event.

There is a time and a place for both.

---

| | Cold email | Creative outbound |
|---|---|---|

| Cost | Around $255 a month for inboxes, infrastructure and tools | At least a couple of thousand dollars per account |
| Best for | Daily lead flow, keeping the calendar full | Landing the logos that change your business |

---

# Why the whale is worth the extra cost

Say it normally costs you **$1,500** to win a client worth **$20,000**.

Going after a whale like Netflix might cost **5x more: $7,500**. But the lifetime value can **10x to $200,000**.

To make $200,000 from small fish you need 10 clients at $1,500 each = **$15,000** to win them. One whale at $7,500 is half that. When you stack up the economics, the whale wins.

People don't make these plays because they have to put up the extra money first. Look at the long run, not the short term.

---

# Where each one fails

**Cold email fails at signing whales.** Big companies are whales for a reason: everyone wants to work with them. The person who goes above and beyond is the one who wins them.

**Creative outbound fails at daily lead flow.** As Hormozi says, don't use a harpoon to catch fish. Spending $7,500 to land a $20,000 deal you could win for $1,500 makes no sense.

---

- **Dialled-in sales process, real case studies, and you know you can deliver?** Add creative outbound on top and go get whales.
