# What 365 Days of Studying Outreach Taught Me

The best lessons from a year of books, resources and testing, so you can book meetings with anyone you want.

---

# Who this is for

Anyone who wants replies from big prospects, not more ignored messages.

**Who am I?** I'm Dan James. I've signed lots of whale clients with creative outreach movies and physical goods, and booked meetings with companies like ListKit.

---

# Lesson 1: Outreach is now more like romance than business

Anyone can send outreach now, so you can't treat prospects like names on a list. Cold email is swiping left and right on a dating app. Nobody feels special.

Treat it like taking your partner on a trip to the Maldives instead: research them, understand them as a person, and understand their short-term goals, long-term goals and their business. **Treat your prospect like a lover, not a dollar, and they'll love you for it.**

---

# Lesson 2: Cold email will keep getting worse

The whole cold email process can be automated by machines. As AI improves it gets easier, more people do it, inboxes fill up and conversion rates keep dropping. Clients don't want to be swiped on. They want special treatment.

---

# Lesson 3: Make it all about the prospect

"Hi, I'm Bill from mortgagebrokers.org and me and the team in Florida do this." I don't care, Bill. What can you do for me?

Outreach asks for a commitment: time, energy and eventually money. So tell them exactly how you'll help. Lead with a no-brainer offer (Hormozi's value equation: the dream outcome, how likely they are to get it, how fast, and how little effort it takes).

**Bad:** "I'm a personal trainer, I'm jacked and we hit the gym."

**Good:** "Hey Ben, we help men just like you get into incredible shape while they run their business. We've helped 1,000+ people this year. Is this a goal for you in 2027, or not for you?"

Only talk about yourself in the context of how you help them.

---

# Lesson 4: 99% of outbound dies at research

All outreach says: **we see problem X, here's the result of solving it, here's why we're the best people to solve it, here's how, and here are the people we've done it for.** If you can't name the problem, why are you reaching out?

For whales, understand them as a person, as a business, and their goals over the next five weeks, five months and five years. Then lay out the roadmap. When people say my outreach is creative, it's really the research. The research is where the magic is done.

---

# Lesson 5: Follow-ups must be intentional

"Just bumping this" or "please read my message" is insane. Every follow-up should add value: a new angle, more resources, something you spotted about their business. Make them think "damn, this is valuable."

As Jill Konrath explains in *How to Sell to Big Companies*, the deeper you understand a business, the easier it is to sell to them. Your research is a Bentley in the garage. Use it.
